Understanding Pearl Millet (Bajra)

A familiar grain with a place in many regional kitchens, and a few practical notes for cooking with its flour.

22 September 2026By Vraj Naturals EditorialFood & Nutrition
Home/Journal/Pearl Millet

Pearl millet, commonly called bajra in India, is a cereal grain used in foods across parts of South Asia and Africa. It is sold in different forms, including whole grain and flour. The exact taste, texture and cooking behaviour depend on the product and how it has been processed.

From grain to flour

Bajra flour is made by milling pearl millet. Like other flours, it can vary in fineness and freshness. Check the ingredient list and packing details, and keep the flour in a clean, dry, closed container after opening.

Getting started in the kitchen

Bajra flour is used for flatbreads and other regional preparations. Dough made from millet flour may handle differently from wheat dough, so start with a small batch and add water gradually. Some cooks shape the dough by hand or use a clean sheet to help flatten it. Follow a recipe that suits the texture you want.

Adding variety thoughtfully

Grains can contribute variety to meals, but nutrition depends on the overall diet, portion sizes and preparation. No one grain is a cure or a substitute for personalised medical advice. If you follow a specific dietary plan or have an allergy or medical condition, consult a qualified professional and check product handling information.

Make the label part of the choice

Look for a clear product name, ingredient list, net quantity, dates and seller contact information. If you need to know where a batch was grown or milled, ask the seller. These practical details help you decide whether a product fits your household and your cooking.

IN YOUR KITCHEN

Try a small recipe first and adjust water to the particular flour you have.
